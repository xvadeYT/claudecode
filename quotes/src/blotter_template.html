Posting times

Why these times. Midday catches the lunch scroll, and the evening slot lands just before the 8–10 PM peak, when teens and young adults spend the most time on Reels. Posting a little before the peak means your reel is already out when the first wave of viewers shows up. That first wave is who Instagram tests it on.

After two weeks, check your own numbers. Go to Professional dashboard → Total followers → Most active times, and move these to match your audience. Every date and time in the list updates.

Times are in your phone's time zone. What gets a reel pushed is the first 3 seconds, watch time, and how many people send it or save it. The time you post only helps it get seen at the start.

 

Two reels a day for 500 days. Files render to Downloads\REELS_GENZ_V4\reel_0001.mp4 … reel_1000.mp4 from QUOTES_GENZ_V4.txt, where line 1 is reel_0001. Each reel has a matching cover: cover_0001.jpg … cover_1000.jpg.

In Instagram: pick the reel → add music → Edit cover → Add from camera roll and pick the matching cover_NNNN.jpg → tap Copy here and paste → More options → Schedule this Reel at the time shown. Instagram lets you schedule up to 75 days ahead, so do it a few weeks at a time.

Posted taps save to your account, so your progress follows you to any device.
